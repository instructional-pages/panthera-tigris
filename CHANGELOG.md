# Changelog

All notable changes to this course are documented here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

## [0.15.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 14, *Counting tigers, and saving them*: thirteen steps. Junu, twelve, is photographed by
  the national count's cameras. The Red List and why the tiger is Endangered; capture–recapture
  by stripes; the world's 3,726–5,578 wild tigers; India's and Nepal's counts over time beside
  the world estimate; why a higher count is not always more tigers, and South-East Asia's
  losses; the threats in order; why tigers can't absorb much killing (the 15% line); the 2010
  goal to double numbers; protection, corridors and reintroduction.
- Two checks: estimate a population from two camera sessions (20, 15 and 6 seen twice), and
  predict whether a population with plentiful prey but 20% deaths a year recovers, each locked
  in before its answer is taught.
- Two sandboxes: a camera-trap survey of a model reserve (4, 9 or 16 cameras, spread out or
  bunched; two sessions, the photos matched by stripes, the estimate, then the true number);
  and a population model (prey and yearly deaths) that shows whether 20 tigers recover over 40
  years. Both say which of their numbers are made up.

### Changed

- The bundle budget is 135 kB gzipped for the whole site, up from 120 kB, to fit lesson 14 and
  the final quiz.

## [0.14.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 13, *The tiger in culture*: twelve steps. Junu, ten, lives beside villages with their own
  ways of living with her. China's king of beasts, white tiger of the west and protector of
  ordinary people; emperors, Buddhist art and Korea's tribute skins; Durga's great cat, often a
  tiger; Waghoba, Bonbibi and the Dibang Valley's kinship; Sumatra's ancestral tigers; Tipu's
  Tiger, Thai talismans and Singapore's bounty and brands; Blake and Kipling; Project Tiger and
  the flagship; and how belief can support tolerance. Beliefs are told as the people's own
  account.
- A check: match five stories to the places they come from (six places, one left over), locked
  in before any story is told.
- A sandbox: a culture map of ten places, each with how people see the tiger, what they do, and
  what the studies read found about tolerance there.

## [0.13.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 12, *The trade in tiger parts*: eleven steps. Junu, nine, has rangers remove a snare
  near her range. Why tigers are bought (medicine, status, belief, meat) and where the demand is;
  the prices along the chain from poacher to buyer; CITES and Appendix I; weak penalties; what
  2,359 seized tigers show; captive tigers and the evidence against farms; and South Korea's ban.
  Factual, never graphic.
- A check: set a slider to the poacher's share of the final price, locked in before any price is
  shown. The shared estimate slider gains an "at most" answer, for sources that give a bound.
- A sandbox: TRAFFIC's seizure data, 2000–2018, by year (incidents or tigers, with or without the
  2016 temple seizure), by country (yearly averages in 2000–2014 and 2015–2018) or by part.
- The dossier's trade file gains the report's figures behind its totals, by year, country and
  part.

## [0.12.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 11, *Tigers and people*: twelve steps. Junu, eight, has a range that reaches into the
  buffer zone, where villagers cut grass. Human–tiger conflict, livestock depredation and
  retaliatory killing; how many attacks are recorded (Nepal's lowlands, Chitwan); where and when
  (buffer zones, forests, by day); who is at risk (people alone, people bending or sitting); which
  tigers attack (old, injured, dispersing); why conflict rises as tigers recover; livestock, and
  how a tiger near a Bhutanese village protected farms; tolerance; and what helps. Factual,
  never graphic.
- A check: two pairs, which carried more risk (inside parks or in buffer zones; bending or
  standing when attacked), locked in before the study's figures are shown.
- A sandbox: put a villager on a drawn buffer-zone map, choose where they are, whether they are
  alone and what they are doing, and see which way each choice moves the risk with the study's
  figure, and a ladder that orders the choices without claiming a measured risk.

## [0.11.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 10, *Prey, rivals and the forest*: nine steps. Junu, seven, shares her range with a
  leopard. Tigers as habitat generalists that need large prey; density per 100 km², from 15–19 in
  Kaziranga and Corbett to 0.13–0.45 in Sikhote-Alin, drawn on a stretched scale; why prey sets the
  number of tigers, as a simple model; India's 11 sites, where more prey meant more tigers; home
  ranges from about 20 km² in Chitwan to 400 km² in the Russian Far East, to scale; and tigers,
  leopards and dholes sharing the forest when prey is plentiful and splitting it when it is
  scarce, with Rajaji's tigers and leopards before and after.
- A check: given 20 deer per km², 1 in 10 taken a year and 50 kills a tiger, set how many tigers
  100 km² feeds, before the model is run.
- A sandbox: a prey slider that sets the model forest's tigers per 100 km² (drawn in a 10 km
  square), the forest per tiger and what leopards do, with the model's made-up share spared (5%,
  10% or 20%) to change, beside the measured densities.

## [0.10.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 9, *Where tigers live*: eight steps. Junu, six, is one small dot on the map of the
  tiger's old range, from Turkey to Bali: 30 countries, 11.1 million km². The losses decade by
  decade, from Singapore in the 1930s to Laos about 2014, with Java's disputed date; the three
  layers of why (habitat gone, unusable, emptied by killing); the 63 landscapes left, less than a
  tenth of the old range, by country; and the trend since 2001, with 226 Restoration Landscapes.
- A check: pick the ten places that still have breeding tigers on the map before the losses are
  told.
- A sandbox: a slider from 1900 to 2020 that empties the map decade by decade, naming what was
  lost, with a toggle for the three layers of loss.

## [0.9.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 8, *How many kinds of tiger?*: nine steps. Junu, five, is a Bengal tiger, but is that a
  subspecies? Meet the nine named populations, the older eight subspecies, the evidence the 2017
  IUCN revision weighed, its two subspecies (mainland and Sunda Islands), the 2018 genome study's
  six living groups, and why both answers can be defended.
- A check: put each living population into *P. t. tigris* or *P. t. sondaica* before the two
  are shown.
- A sandbox: a model tree of the nine populations with made-up differences, ordered to match the
  sources. Move the line for how big a difference counts, and watch the count go from nine to one,
  through the genomes' six and the revision's two.

### Changed

- The bundle budget is 120 kB gzipped for the whole site, up from 60 kB: each lesson is its own
  chunk, so one visit downloads about 19 kB.

## [0.8.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 7, *A life: mating, cubs, leaving home*: nine steps. Junu, nearly four, has her first
  litter, and her brother's very different path is followed too. Learn about heat and induced
  ovulation, the mating months, a pregnancy of about 100 days and two years of care, the stages
  of growing up on a time line from the sources, why daughters settle close and sons go far, and
  how many cubs live to leave home.
- Two checks: put the stages of a young female's life in order, and set a slider to how many of
  ten Chitwan cubs are alive at two years, each before the lesson tells you.
- A sandbox: follow 10 or 100 cubs, half daughters and half sons, through the stages with the
  studies' survival figures, run it again and again, and switch the first year to the Russian Far
  East's to see what heavy killing by people does.
- Research: Kerley et al. 2003 (litters, birth intervals and cub deaths in the Russian Far East)
  and the Cat Specialist Group's Chitwan figures fill lesson 7's gap in the dossier.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 6, *A territory of her own*: eight steps. Junu, two years old, leaves her mother and
  settles next door on part of her mother's old range. Learn what a home range and a territory
  are, how solitary tigers stay in touch, the five ways they mark, why marks gather at the edges
  of a territory and where travel routes cross them, and how neighbouring females share a
  boundary by visiting it at different times.
- A check: leave Junu's five scent marks on a map of her range, then see where tigers really mark
  most, before the lesson tells you.
- A sandbox: walk Junu through her range a step at a time or a thousand at once, watch her marks
  build up at the edges, compare where she spends her time with where she marks, and turn on the
  "odour field" of the other tigers' marks to see where a meeting is likely.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 5, *Hunting*: nine steps. Junu, eighteen months old, hunts beside her mother. Learn how
  an ambush hunter works; what Chitwan's tigers eat, laid out by body mass; why "eaten most" is
  not "preferred", with the 60–250 kg band tigers prefer range-wide and the Chitwan exceptions; and
  how GPS collars find kills.
- Two checks: put the steps of a hunt in order, and set a slider to the days between kills
  (about 6.5), both before the answer is told.
- A sandbox: compare the days between kills and the meat eaten per day in summer, in winter, and
  after a small or a large kill, against the year's average.
- A new source, a 2017 study of Chitwan tigers' diet, in the research dossier.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 4, *Voice and senses*: eight steps. Junu, a year old, answers her mother's call in tall
  grass. See a tiger's roar (83–246 Hz, average 158 Hz) on a pitch scale beside human speech, and
  why the fat in its vocal folds is not there to lower the pitch; the cochleas of a house cat, a
  person and a tiger drawn uncoiled to scale; scent marks as long-lasting chemical messages; and
  why pupil shape follows hunting style and height.
- The lesson's check: predict whether a tiger's ear is tuned more like a house cat's or a
  person's, then see the answer and how it was measured.
- A sandbox built on a study of 214 animals: choose a hunting style and a height and see which
  pupil shape the data predicts, then place a house cat and a tiger.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 3, *The body: size, coat, stripes*: twelve steps. Junu, six months old, already has her
  own stripes. See the largest tiger (an Amur male) and the smallest (a Sumatran) drawn to scale
  beside a person; why no two stripe patterns are the same; how camera traps tell tigers apart
  and count them; melanin, genes, recessive copies and carriers; white tigers; and the
  "black" (pseudomelanistic) tigers of Similipal, and why genetic drift made them common there.
- The lesson's check: match four camera-trap photos, each showing part of a tiger's side, to
  three tigers, then see which matches were right.
- Two sandboxes: breed cubs from parents with zero, one or two copies of the white or "black"
  coat's gene, and see how many show it; then follow a rare copy through 20 isolated
  populations at once, small or large, and see where chance alone makes it common.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 2, *Two and a half million years of tigers*: eleven steps beside a time line from
  10.8 million years ago to today, drawn so each tick is ten times older than the next. Junu, two
  months old, starts to travel with her mother. Learn Mya and kya, the Pleistocene, the Longdan
  skull (the oldest member of the tiger's line), the trend to larger size, mitochondrial DNA and
  the most recent common ancestor, the bottleneck about 100,000 years ago, and why the Toba
  eruption is a hypothesis, not a proven cause.
- The lesson's check: set a slider to when the last common ancestor of all living tigers lived,
  then see the answer beside your estimate, and your estimate on the time line.
- The lesson's sandbox: a model of maternal lines. Pick a population size, run generations, and
  watch lines die out until every female shares one founding mother; a bottleneck makes it
  happen fast. Your runs are listed side by side.
- A list of the lessons at the top of every page, and a link to the next lesson on a lesson's
  last step. Each lesson's code loads only when it is opened.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- Lesson 1, *What is a tiger?*: eight steps beside a family tree of the cats that you open branch
  by branch. Meet Junu, a fictional tiger cub born in Chitwan, Nepal; learn species, genus,
  family and subspecies, and how to read a family tree.
- The lesson's check: predict where the tiger and the snow leopard sit in the big-cat tree before
  the answer is shown, including why one well-known zoo says the lion instead.
- The lesson's sandbox: group the cats by a trait (spots, size, where they live) or by hand, and
  see whether the tree makes the group one whole branch.
- Numbered citations on every fact, linked to a source list at the foot of the page.
- The lesson outline (`docs/LESSONS.md`): 14 lessons and a final quiz, approved by the owner.
- In the dossier: the other pantherine cats' weights, coats and ranges, from the Cat Specialist
  Group's species pages.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- The site skeleton: a dark, quiet placeholder page with the step-by-step Back/Next pattern
  that every lesson will use, working from the keyboard.
- A note on the page that the course is researched and written with Claude (AI).
- The research dossier in `docs/research/`: 13 cited topics, from taxonomy to culture.
